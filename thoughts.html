Why most AI products feel like uncanny valley software.

"A textarea with attitude" is not a product. It's a placeholder. The good ones treat the model as a collaborator, not a paperclip mascot — here's what that actually means in interface terms.

There is a specific feeling you get when you use a bad AI product. The interface looks fine. The features make sense on paper. But something about the experience is faintly off — like the software is wearing a costume of itself.

Most of the time, this is because the AI was bolted on. Designed for a world without it, then asked to accommodate one with it. The seams show. The chat interface lives in a sidebar. The "powered by AI" badge is doing real emotional labor.

The good AI products don't have that problem. They feel like the AI was always going to be there — like the designer started with the model and worked outward, not inward. Cursor. Linear's auto-categorization. Notion's AI when it's tuned right. The interface and the intelligence were designed together, not stapled together.

The trick is that AI isn't a feature you add. It's a material you design with — like color, like type, like motion. And like any material, it has a grain. Working with the grain feels natural. Working against it feels uncanny.
