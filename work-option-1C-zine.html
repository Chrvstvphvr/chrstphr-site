A Mechanic Made of Manuals, Mostly.

How one designer with no garage experience built a multi-modal RAG sidekick to keep a 1973 Corvette on the road — and ate $6,800 in shop labor in the process.

ClientPersonal
Year2025 → now
RoleSolo Builder
StackFastAPI · Claude · pgvector
Filed underAI · Full-Stack
Read time~ 4 min

The dream car arrived first. The competence to keep it running did not. Factory service manuals from 1972 are dense, opinionated, and 400 pages long. Forum threads are gold but unsearchable. The shop quotes were brutal educational.

So this designer did the only reasonable thing: built a multi-modal RAG sidekick from scratch. FastAPI on top, Claude in the middle, Supabase + pgvector underneath. Ingested gigabytes of manuals, threads, and repair logs. Now the answer fits in a pocket.

"It tells you what it is, what it does, and when it was last replaced."

The multi-modal layer is the thing. Type a description of a problem and you'll get a fine answer. Photograph it and you'll get a great one. The model identifies the component, cross-references the manual, and surfaces the relevant repair procedure, with citation.

Lessons: domain expertise doesn't have to live in a person. Structure the knowledge correctly — chunked, embedded, retrievable — and the model becomes genuinely expert. Not a chatbot. An expert. Photos are magic; everything else is just text.

¹ The Corvette runs. The shop quotes do not. The author is, as ever, available for the right things.

What Your Texting Really Says About You.

A wrapped macOS application analyzing nearly a quarter-million messages over more than a decade. Sentiment, topic modeling, and an AI chat tab. Most active at 11am. And 2am.

ClientPersonal
Year2026
RoleSolo
StackPyQt6 · Claude · NLP
Filed underAI · Desktop
Sample246,000 msgs

Twelve years of iMessage history loaded into a single PyQt6 app. The promise was simple: hand it to a friend without a single line of explanation, and let them learn something about themselves. 20+ insights surface automatically.

Sentiment analysis runs on every conversation. Topic modeling clusters who you talk to and what about. An AI chat tab lets you ask the corpus questions in plain language: "who do I argue with most?", "when did I stop texting Dad as much?", "summarize 2018."

Twelve years of group chats. One desktop app. No regrets.

The hardest part wasn't the analysis. It was the wrapping: making it feel like a real macOS app, not a Python script with a UI taped on. PyQt6 helped. So did 14 hours of caffeine.

² All processing happens locally. Your texts stay on your machine. The author would not have it any other way.

The Pizza Shop That Still Runs on Paper.

A shop that refused a kitchen display system gets vision recognition and a Twilio text loop instead. Tickets stay paper. Customers get a text. Two displays show the live queue.

ClientBrenz Pizza Co
YearJan 2026
RoleSolo Builder
StackVision · Twilio · Web
Filed underUX/UI · Vision
Read time~ 3 min

The brief was unusual: do not replace the paper tickets. Replace nothing the staff likes. Just make the rest of the system smarter around them. The kitchen had refused a KDS three times. The owner refused, on principle, to refuse a fourth.

The fix: a camera reads the paper ticket at each stage of the make process. The system knows where every order is in real time. Customers get a Twilio text with their ETA. Two live displays show the queue — one in the dining room, one visible from the parking lot. Staff override anything from a single dashboard.

Don't replace the paper. Replace the not-knowing.

The lesson, writ large: most "digital transformations" fail because they replace the part of the workflow people actually love. Replace the part they hate. Then you've helped.

³ Tickets are still paper. The staff are still happy. The owner is still smug.

The Scoreboard the Boss Asked For on a Tuesday.

A real-time operational dashboard tracking sales, labor, transactions, voids — across seven shops. Designed, built, deployed in three hours of an unusually focused Tuesday afternoon.

ClientBrenz Pizza Co
YearMar 2026
RoleSolo Builder
StackReal-time · Claude Code
Filed underUX/UI · Dashboard
Time to ship3 hours

The boss asked on a Tuesday. The scoreboard was live by Tuesday evening. Every figure across seven locations on one dark display. Sales, labor, transactions, voids. Real-time, refreshed automatically, designed to be glanceable from across a room.

This is what good tools feel like, eventually: a single surface that shows you the truth of the operation in under a second. Currently in use. Currently being argued about. Currently keeping the boss honest.

The boss asked. The boss got. The boss now checks it hourly.

If there's a takeaway: ship the scoreboard before the meeting. The conversation that follows is always better.

⁴ Yes, it was three hours. No, the team is not happy that it was three hours. The designer is.
