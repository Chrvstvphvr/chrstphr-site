The Receipt App That Argues Back.

01 / Brief

The real brief.

The client came in with a clean ask: "We want to redesign our budgeting app to be more friendly." Standard request. Most designers would have shipped a softer color palette and called it.

The real problem surfaced in user interviews. People didn't want a friendly budgeting app. They wanted one that called them out — gently — when they were lying to themselves about their spending. The AI was already there. It just wasn't being trusted to push back.

02 / Approach

Designing a collaborator, not an interface.

We rebuilt the AI's role from "summarizer" to "collaborator with a stake." The model now had three escalating tones: curious, skeptical, and direct. Each one mapped to a clear UI state — not a chat sidebar, but woven into the spending feed itself.

"It feels like it cares whether I figure this out."

The biggest design decision was making the AI visibly hesitate. When the model wasn't confident, the interface acknowledged that. When it was sure, the language got tighter. Anthropomorphism is a bug. Honest anthropomorphism is, sometimes, a feature.

03 / Reflection

What CHRSTPHR learned.

The biggest lesson: AI products fail when the designer treats the model as a feature instead of a co-author. Once we stopped trying to make the AI "polite" and let it disagree productively, the product had something the budget category was missing — stakes.

If CHRSTPHR did this again: more time on the model's tone calibration, less on the empty-state illustrations. Nobody screenshots an empty state.
